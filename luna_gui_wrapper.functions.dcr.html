luna_gui_wrapper.functions.dcr module

Dark count rate (DCR) of one HDF5 file, per pixel and per cluster.

One pass over PixelHits and one over Clusters counts hits and clusters per pixel and per frame; the rates follow by dividing by a measurement time. That time is the one real choice, so there are four, all computed and shown side by side:

  • frames_toa – live time from the hits themselves: ToA is sorted, so the

    N-1 largest ToA gaps are the dead gaps between N frames (N = entries in /ExposureTimeBoundaries). Live time is the first-to-last span minus those gaps. Needs no metadata.

  • frames_meta – frames x ExposureTime from measurement_metadata.json.

  • toa_span – first to last hit. Counts the dead gaps between frames as

    live, so it runs ~1 % long for 0.495 s frames every 0.5 s.

  • wall_clock – StartDateTime to StopDateTime from the metadata. Includes

    start-up and readout overhead; an upper bound.

/ExposureTimeBoundaries holds no times: it is one cumulative row index per frame. Those indices do not always match PixelHits (a file whose last boundary lies past the end of the table exists), so only their count is used; the frame edges come from the ToA gaps.

Noisy pixels are those with more than k x the median hit count of the pixels that counted at all. Silent pixels counted nothing – masked or dead when the median is high, possibly just Poisson zeros when it is a few hits. The “clean” rates leave both out: noisy pixels from the count, and both from the area.

Qt-free. numpy is imported inside the functions that need it.

luna_gui_wrapper.functions.dcr.interp_median(c, np)[source]

Median of integer counts, interpolated within the middle value.

Per frame a pixel holds a few tens of counts, so the plain median moves in whole counts – a ~3 % staircase that hides a 0.3 % drift. Treating each integer v as the interval [v - 0.5, v + 0.5) and interpolating (the grouped-data median) gives a continuous estimate; it equals v when every count is v.
